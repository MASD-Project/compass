* Goal

ORE Studio's MSVC build fails with =C1202: recursive type or function
dependency context too complex=, after the push and only on Windows. In
the failed-build investigation it is the pattern "MSVC reflection depth
limit": 50 runs and 6,076 CI minutes, classified from the error alone. A
developer on Linux or macOS cannot see it coming. compass should report
it before the push, from the source.

Two limits meet in that one error, and this story treats them apart:

- *A struct too large to reflect.* One type whose reflection alone
  passes the limit. The bond export carrier of 2026-10-03 is one:
  reflect-cpp writing a tagged variant builds one literal over every
  field name reachable from the alternative, and the bond carrier's was
  too long. What counts is what one instantiation reaches, not a
  struct's member count: ORE Studio abandoned a nine-member ceiling
  because 220 of its 287 generated reflected structs pass it and
  compile.
- *Reflection depth.* The instantiation context a translation unit
  builds up, which MSVC appears to bound per unit. The trade entity
  failure of 2026-11-19 is one: =trade_entity.hpp:95= stops on the
  =performed_by= binding of a 19-binding column list, on a context two
  notes deep, late in a unity unit of 57 entity headers. A unity build
  puts 16 sources in one unit (=ORES_UNITY_BUILD_BATCH_SIZE=), so the
  entity that fails is whichever comes late in a heavy unit, not the
  largest. The check
  must compute the depth a unit accumulates, which needs a model of how
  MSVC counts.

Neither limit is documented by Microsoft as a number, so the model is
measured: probes compiled with MSVC on compass's own Windows CI, sized
until C1202 fires, give each mechanism's ceiling and say whether a
unit's instantiations add up.

* Acceptance

- Every C1202 failure of the investigation is explained by a mechanism,
  with its unit and the type it stopped on.
- MSVC's ceiling for each mechanism, and whether a unit's instantiations
  accumulate, are measured on MSVC, with the probes in the repository.
- A check reports a struct too large to reflect, and fires on a past
  failure of that kind and is quiet on its fix.
- A check computes each translation unit's and unity batch's reflection
  depth and reports one over the limit; it fires on the trade entity
  failure and is quiet on its fix.

* Decisions

- *Measure the limit on MSVC, once, and compute it from source after.*
  MSVC's C1202 limit is not documented. Probes on compass's Windows CI
  (=.github/workflows/msvc-probes.yml=, run by hand) measured it; the
  checks compute the measured rule from source and need no MSVC.
- *The rule is per unit and counts chains.* A unit fails at the first
  =operator|= of a column-list chain that is the 1,026th of the unit or
  later and holds 18 bindings or more. It predicts all 337 chain probes,
  ORE Studio's own entity headers among them
  (=build/probes/msvc_c1202/model.py=).
- *No single struct fails alone in today's ORE Studio.* reflect-cpp, the
  cause of the 81 errors of one type too large, left ORE Studio on
  2026-11-09. A column list alone compiles at every width probed, up to
  600, and a field list at any width or count.
- *The cure is a flat column list.* Built in one call, as field lists
  are, column lists never failed; it is ORE Studio's generator to
  change. The check stays, for chains a project keeps.

* Out of scope

- Fixing ORE Studio's failing types. The checks say which; ORE Studio
  splits them.
- Clang's and GCC's limits. They have not failed ORE Studio's build.
